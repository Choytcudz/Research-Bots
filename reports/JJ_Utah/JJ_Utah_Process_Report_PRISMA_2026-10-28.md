## 1A. Methodological framework (literature-supported)

**Reporting standard.** The flow in Section 4 follows the PRISMA 2020 statement (Page, McKenzie, et al., 2021) and its explanation and elaboration paper (Page, Moher, et al., 2021). The search report in Section 3 follows the PRISMA-S extension for reporting literature searches (Rethlefsen et al., 2021). PRISMA was written for systematic reviews of studies. Here it is applied to a corpus of primary legal and institutional documents, so every departure from the standard is stated in Section 4A rather than hidden.

**Grey literature and agency documents.** Agency policies, interagency agreements, and published administrative decisions are grey literature: material produced outside commercial and academic publishing. Systematic searching of grey literature calls for documented, reproducible strategies across agency websites and targeted web searches, with the limits of web search engines (ranking, personalization, and changing results) reported openly (Adams et al., 2016; Godin et al., 2015; Paez, 2017). Those sources are why Section 3 records the web searches verbatim and Section 7 states that hit counts were not recorded and that results are not reproducible.

**Document analysis.** Document analysis is a systematic procedure for finding, selecting, appraising, and synthesizing the data held in documents, and it requires the researcher to judge each document's authenticity, credibility, and representativeness and to record its provenance (Bowen, 2009). The four checks (the source exists, the citation leads to it, the provision says what is claimed, and the source is placed in the authority hierarchy) and the capture dates put that appraisal into operation.

**Doctrinal legal research.** Locating the primary sources of law, confirming that they are current, and placing each in the hierarchy of authority before analyzing it is the core of doctrinal legal method (Hutchinson & Duncan, 2012). That method supports the currency checks (for example, the July 1, 2027 repeal under Utah Code § 63I-1-253) and the rule that a secondary copy (the Cornell LII copy of R547) cannot stand in for official text.

**Constructivist grounded theory and coding.** The brief calls for gerund coding, constant comparison, and memo writing in the constructivist grounded theory tradition (Charmaz, 2006), and the matching coding practice is described by Saldaña (2016). The disability critical race studies lens (DisCrit) is one of the alternative readings the brief names (Annamma et al., 2013). These frameworks guided the plan only; I wrote no coded memos (Section 2), so no analytic claim in this report rests on them.

**Audit trail.** The live, time-stamped audit log and the corrections appended without editing history form an audit trail, the dependability and confirmability device in naturalistic inquiry (Lincoln & Guba, 1985). Appendix A reproduces that trail verbatim.

## 6. Synthesis of leads (descriptive; not findings)

1. The state education agency is "directly responsible" for the education of individuals "being held in a juvenile detention facility" (Utah Code § 53E-3-503(1)(a)(ii)(C)). Whether that phrase reaches DJJYS secure care is an open question.
2. The evaluation-on-request duty names only the Division of Child and Family Services (§ 53E-7-207(3)(a); SpEd Rules II.B.2). I found no DJJYS counterpart in those texts.
3. The SpEd Rules apply by their own terms to "State and local juvenile and adult correctional facilities" (I.C.1.e), and IV.U.2 provides for rights to transfer at incarceration.
4. The public DJJYS policy index shows no policy titled education, special education, IEP, or surrogate parent. DJJYS 04-01 III.D.10 gives a right to "access to public education ... (as applicable)." This absence is a lead only, and 01-07 III.E indicates the operations manuals may be on the Division's website.
5. The coordinating council in § 53E-3-503(5) and (6), which R277-709-8 relies on, is scheduled for repeal on July 1, 2027 (§ 63I-1-253(19) and (20)).
6. The only public interagency MOU located (DHS, USOE and the Juvenile Court) ran from 2014 to July 1, 2019. Whether it has a successor, and whether the SpEd VI.K.4 MOU is public, is unknown.
